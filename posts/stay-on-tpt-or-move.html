Should You Move to an Alternative Platform or Stay on TpT?

TpT has announced a new Exclusive Seller membership, and the seller community has been talking about little else. Some sellers are worried, some are angry, and some are quietly opening stores on other platforms. This article puts the facts, the community's reactions and the alternatives in one place, so you can decide with a clear head.

What TpT announced

According to TpT's seller blog and help center, the Exclusive Seller membership replaces the Premium Seller membership starting January 1, 2027, and the deadline to register is December 31, 2026. TpT has also scheduled a town hall webinar for October 8, 2026.

The key point is the trade-off. Exclusive sellers agree to sell their digital educational resources only through TpT. They can still promote elsewhere, such as on a website, social media or an email list, as long as purchases go through TpT. Free resources and some non-educational products (physical products, courses, Boom Cards on Boom, Kindle books) can still be sold elsewhere. Basic sellers can keep selling on other platforms.

What sellers are saying

In seller communities the reactions fall into three groups.

"This takes away my freedom"

Many creators have spent years building businesses beyond TpT: their own websites, email lists, memberships and other marketplaces. As they understand it, TpT is not saying you can't sell elsewhere. It is saying the benefits go to sellers who agree to exclusivity. Stay non-exclusive and you keep your freedom but give up some of those benefits. For sellers with a real business off TpT, that choice feels like a penalty, and that is why the conversation is so loud.

"TpT needs us more than we need TpT"

Other sellers take a firmer line. Without the resources teacher-authors create, the marketplace has nothing to sell, and authors are free to sell anywhere. Teachers shop on TpT today, they argue, but that can change, and sellers can pivot.

"I'm already testing other platforms"

Some sellers are quietly diversifying. One seller already has stores on TpT, Tes, Classful and Teach Simple, and is now trying Amped Up Learning. Their questions are the ones everyone has: how are the sales, how is the traffic, and how easy is the store to manage? Honest answers are still hard to find, so if you have tried one of these platforms, share what you learned.

The alternatives at a glance

  • Tes Resources. A long-running marketplace for teacher-made resources. One 2026 guide reports that Tes keeps 45% of each sale, so sellers keep 55%. Check their current terms.
  • Classful. A marketplace where educators open shops and sell resources. Classful states that it charges a 5% fee per transaction, with no platform or membership cost.
  • Teach Simple. A marketplace with thousands of worksheets, activities and lesson plans, which invites teachers to become contributors. Check the contributor terms before you upload.
  • Amped Up Learning. A marketplace with many teacher contributor stores across subjects and grade levels. The pages I looked at don't list the revenue split, so ask before you commit.
  • Your own website and email list. The only audience you truly own. Basic sellers can sell from their own site, while Exclusive sellers can only promote it and send buyers back to TpT.

Keep in mind that a new platform means a new store to set up, new listings to maintain, and a new audience to build. TpT is still where most teachers go to search today, and a few sellers admit this openly.

Stay, split or move?

1. Go Exclusive

You get the higher payout and the advanced tools. It makes the most sense if most of your income already comes from TpT and you don't sell digital educational resources anywhere else.

2. Stay Basic and diversify

You keep TpT's traffic and your freedom, but accept the lower payout. It makes sense if you already have meaningful sales or an audience off TpT.

3. Move most of your business elsewhere

This lowers your dependence on one company, but you risk losing the search traffic TpT brings. It only works if you can bring your own audience.

Do the math with your own numbers. Take a simplified example of $1,000 per month in TpT sales. At 77.5% you keep $775, and at 55% you keep $550, a gap of $225 per month (before per-order fees and the $59.95 annual fee). The question is whether the freedom to sell elsewhere will earn you more than that gap.

Questions to ask yourself

  • What share of my income comes from TpT today?
  • Do I already have an email list, website or sales off TpT?
  • Do I have the time to maintain stores on several platforms?
  • Which of my best sellers would I be willing to keep only on TpT?
  • Do I sell things that Exclusive still allows elsewhere, like courses, physical products or Boom Cards?

What to do this week

  1. Read the official announcement and FAQ (linked below). Don't rely on screenshots or second-hand summaries.
  2. Look at your last 3 to 6 months of sales, split by platform.
  3. Run the payout math for both memberships with your real numbers.
  4. Test before you commit. Put a few best sellers on one alternative platform and track sales and traffic for a couple of months.
  5. Start an email list wherever you sell. It is the one audience that can follow you to any platform.
  6. Join the October 8 town hall if you can, and bring your questions.
